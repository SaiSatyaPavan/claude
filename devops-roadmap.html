Your three courses

What to take and what to skip

Together the three courses hold roughly 100 hours of video. Watched end to end, with hands-on time, that is a 25-hour week for twelve weeks. Cherry-picked as below it is closer to 17 hours a week, which leaves room for the projects.

AWS Certified Solutions Architect Associate (SAA-C03)
Primary, weeks 1 to 5
Take
  • Every section, in order, plus the included practice exam.
  • Add Tutorials Dojo practice sets in weeks 4 and 5. One practice exam is not enough.
  • Confirm the exam code on the AWS certification page when you book. If a newer version is live, the course still covers most of it. Buy practice exams for the live version.
Skip
  • Nothing. The slides PDF is your revision deck for week 5.
Decoding DevOps, From Basics to Advanced Projects with AI
Cherry-pick, weeks 1 to 12
Take
  • Linux and server management, Bash, YAML and JSON, in the daily slot of weeks 1 to 4.
  • Terraform (VPC, modules, backends) and Ansible, weeks 6 and 7.
  • Docker and Python for AWS automation, week 8.
  • GitHub Actions in full and Jenkins at basic level, week 9.
  • Kubernetes, Helm, Prometheus, Grafana, Alertmanager, weeks 10 and 11.
  • The GitOps project with ArgoCD, week 12 stretch.
Skip
  • Its AWS section. The SAA course already covers it.
  • Vagrant. Use EC2 or WSL instead.
  • Maven, Nexus, SonarQube, GitLab CI: one overview lecture each, no labs.
  • CodeCommit, CodeBuild, CodePipeline, Beanstalk: CodeCommit is closed to new customers. One overview lecture, no labs.
  • Loki and Alloy, until Prometheus and Grafana work.
HashiCorp Certified: Terraform Associate and DevOps Projects
Skip until employed
Why
  • Its Terraform content repeats the Decoding DevOps section you do in weeks 6 and 7.
  • The Terraform Associate exam is a weak resume filter. A working VPC repo says more.
  • Return to it for the exam after January, when an employer may pay for it.

Two rules for all three. The course sample apps sit in hundreds of GitHub portfolios, so follow each project once, then rebuild it for a different small app in your own repo with your own README. And use the AI assistants the course shows for speed, but be able to explain every line, because interviewers ask.
