How each lesson works

  1. Overview. A live, animated diagram of the topic and a hands-on simulator. Tap parts to learn what they do.
  2. Missions. Every interview question is a ticket on a preconfigured unit. Work it with real commands. Stuck? Step 1 gives a hint, Step 2 the full answer and a solution you can watch run. Finish with the interview check.
  3. Console lab. One longer shop-floor scenario per lesson that ties the topic together.

Open the Cheat sheet from the top bar any time. It floats over the page, and Use types a command into the console you were working in.

Plan

Your 30-second pitch

"I'm a hands-on troubleshooter with my CCNA and CompTIA Security+. The CCNA means I can handle the network side of server and rack testing, like link problems, VLANs and PXE boot. Security+ means I understand why firmware, data wiping and BMC access have to be tightly controlled. I'm comfortable with hardware and Linux, and I work methodically: isolate one thing at a time, fix the root cause, and document it. I want to bring that to a debug role and grow toward test engineering."

Adjust the hardware and Linux parts so every word is true for you, then say it out loud until it sounds natural, not memorized.

7-day study plan

  1. Day 1, hardware and ESD. Work the Server hardware and Tools lessons: tap every part in the server diagram, solve the missions, then the console labs.
  2. Day 2, boot and BMC. Solve the Boot, BIOS and BMC missions until you can narrate the power-on sequence without notes. Then the stuck-at-POST lab.
  3. Day 3, Linux. Solve every Linux mission with the cheat sheet closed. Install Ubuntu in a free virtual machine and run the same commands for real with sudo.
  4. Day 4, troubleshooting. This lesson matters most. Solve its missions twice, the second time narrating every step out loud, then do 10 scenario questions in Practice.
  5. Day 5, networking and security. Solve the switch missions and the returned-unit missions so you can bring up your CCNA and Security+ naturally.
  6. Day 6, manufacturing and behavioral. Solve the MES and interview-room missions, keep the STAR story from the shift lab, and write 3 more STAR stories from your own life.
  7. Day 7, full mock. Do 15 random questions in Practice, rerun any mission you solved only with Step 2, then go back over everything marked Needs review.

Build hands-on confidence

  • Take apart and rebuild an old desktop: reseat the RAM, remove and refit the CPU cooler, move drives. Boot it with a DIMM missing and see what happens.
  • Search YouTube for "2U rack server teardown" or models like "PowerEdge R760" to see real server internals.
  • For real BMC and RAID practice, older used rack servers (like a Dell R630 or HPE DL360 Gen9) sell cheaply online. They're loud and heavy, so it's optional.

If there's a hands-on test

Some manufacturing interviews include a practical check: naming parts on a server or motherboard, finding a planted fault (an unseated DIMM, an unplugged fan or cable), reading a multimeter, or running basic Linux commands. Put on the ESD strap first without being told, and narrate what you're checking as you go.

On interview day

  • Bring printed copies of your resume and your CCNA and Security+ certificates.
  • Wear business casual with closed-toe shoes, in case they walk you through the production floor.
  • Think out loud on scenario questions. They're grading your process as much as the final answer.
  • If you don't know something, say so and explain how you'd find out: the work instruction, the platform guide, or asking the test engineer. Never guess with confidence.
  • Mention ESD and safety in at least one answer without being asked.
  • Send a short thank-you email the same day.
